# Independent critique

Use review to resolve uncertainty, not to manufacture agreement. A different context can reduce anchoring; a different model provides a different reviewer configuration, not an independent experiment.

## Neutral review package

Provide the objective, stated claim, intended use, protocol, artifact index and relevant constraints. Give the reviewer access to original source passages, code/configuration, raw outputs and the manuscript as applicable. Label unavailable evidence. Do not prefill the critique with the executor's desired verdict.

Ask reviewers to identify:

- Which claims are supported, unsupported or overextended, with artifact locations.
- The strongest simple competing explanation and an affordable way to distinguish it.
- Comparability, information leakage, measurement or selection problems.
- Missing nearest work and whether the alleged distinction is substantive.
- What change would materially improve the conclusion.

Preserve the original review and record the review mode: self-check, separate agent context, different model, or human review. Do not invent an external expert's participation.

## Two review modes

**Development critique:** the researcher and critic can discuss revisions across rounds. Keep decision history; collect new evidence instead of exchanging increasingly confident opinions. A review score can help triage but does not estimate acceptance probability.

**Simulated peer review:** when several independent referee reports are requested, give each the same frozen manuscript/evidence package in separate contexts. Do not expose another referee's comments, numbers, score, or author response. Freeze all reports before preparing a separate editor/author synthesis. Preserve disagreement in that synthesis.

## Resume and validation

A run marked completed still needs artifact checks. Verify reported outputs exist, correspond to the intended inputs/configuration, and contain the cited values. Validate the scientific interpretation separately from process execution. If evidence is missing, return to the earliest unsupported stage and keep the uncertainty explicit.

Review should result in a next action: a targeted check, a discriminating experiment, a narrowed claim, or a ready-to-write decision. Endless model debate without new evidence consumes budget without resolving the question.
